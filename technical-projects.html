Ecology Simulation

JavaScript, HTML Canvas, no dependencies

Run it live · Source (MIT) · runs in your browser, best on a desktop

  • I wanted to see whether a world I could imagine would do anything interesting if I built it. Small square organisms sense, eat, sleep, fight, flee and breed in a 2D world with a day and night cycle. Seeded, so any run can be replayed.
  • The design is mine: eleven inherited genes, senses that shrink at night, a behaviour priority stack, and homes that form at a well-fed sleep, drift toward where an organism eats, and get abandoned after attacks. There is no aggression gene; violence comes from hunger, relative power and opportunity.
  • Claude Code wrote most of the implementation to my direction. I designed it, tuned it and tested it until the ecology held together at any world size, about 900 organisms at 2 ms a tick.
  • Part art piece, part experiment in what AI can build when someone directs it with a clear idea. I'm still playing with it and watching what comes of it.
Software Keyboard Instrument

A prototype I use myself, started October 2026. Six of seven planned milestones are built.

  • Plays six synth engines written from scratch, a sampled orchestra, and modeled pianos and organs live from a MIDI keyboard, with 115 presets.
  • Records performances as multitrack takes, edited in a piano roll (quantize, humanize, tempo map) and exported as MIDI, WAV, MP3, FLAC, or per-track stems.

Open-World Driving Game Prototype

Unreal Engine 5, C++, Blender with Python · playable in the editor, not yet released

A prototype I started in October 2026: drive an electric car through a desert town at night, with traffic and robots in the streets.

  • The town is generated: Python layout scripts place buildings, streets, and props, Blender builds the meshes, and Unreal assembles them with lit interiors, neon signs, and lot lighting.
  • A C++ traffic system drives 69 AI cars through 4 signalized junctions with stop signs, left-turn yielding, and honking. A C++ crowd system moves 206 robots and drones that cross on walk signals and dodge the player.
  • Engine, robot, drone, and ambient sound is synthesized live from vehicle speed, engine RPM, and player position.
  • I set the concept and creative direction, made the design decisions, and tested it by driving. Claude Code produced most of the code, 3D models, and level edits under my direction, working in the Unreal editor and scripting Blender.
GPU Video Editor

TypeScript, Electron, WebGL2 and GLSL, Web Audio, FFmpeg · Windows desktop app

A desktop video editor I use on my own projects, started October 2026. All editing and exporting runs on the graphics card, fully offline.

  • A multi-track timeline with ripple editing, trimming, keyframes, speed changes, reverse, and freeze frames, previewed in real time on the GPU.
  • 19 picture effects written as custom shaders (color, glow, lens, CRT, film grain, LUTs), 12 transitions, titles, and a sound mixer with EQ, compression, reverb, a master limiter, and loudness normalization.
  • Exports to H.264, H.265, WebM, GIF, WAV, or MP3 through the same renderer as the preview, and 136 automated checks confirm the export matches the preview frame for frame. Export on a 20-second 1080p test project went from 3.2 to 43 frames per second.
  • I set the requirements and design and tested every step on my own footage before the next began. Claude Code wrote the code and its 328 unit tests under my direction.
Local AI News Feed

Python, FastAPI, HTMX, SQLite, Ollama with Qwen models · runs locally on Windows

A personal news feed I use daily, started October 2026. It collects AI and tech news and uses a local AI model to sort and rate stories, with no cloud AI services.

  • Checks 47 public news sources on a schedule and merges coverage of the same event into one story, using an embedding model to find matches and the local AI to confirm borderline ones.
  • Places each story in a section, labels it news or discussion, and rates it 1 to 5 stars with a one-sentence reason, learning from my up and down votes. Major stories trigger a desktop alert.
  • I chose the models by testing them: on 20 hand-labeled headlines, the 9B model sorted 17 correctly versus 9 for a 4B model, and duplicate detection matched my judgment on 17 of 18 pairs.
  • I defined the requirements and design and tested it through daily use. It was built with AI-assisted development using Claude Code, and 138 automated tests pass.
