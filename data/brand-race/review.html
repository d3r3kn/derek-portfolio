Decisions needed

1. Tesla's MPG is 119

EPA counts electric vehicles in its MPG averages using an energy-equivalent figure, so Tesla's 2025 "real-world MPG" is 119.4, about four times every other brand (next best: BMW, 31.1). Tesla will be #1 in MPG every year it appears, and on a chart scale it squashes everyone else together. BMW's and Mercedes' MPG are also lifted by their EV and plug-in hybrid sales.

Options: (a) keep it as EPA reports it, with a note and a broken axis; (b) show Tesla in the MPG ranking but label it "EV, energy-equivalent"; (c) leave Tesla out of the MPG ranking only.

2. How to label 2025

EPA: model year 2025 data "are preliminary", based on automakers' projected production before the cars went on sale. 1975–2024 are final. The 2025 rows have averages but no production counts. Options: label the column "2025 (prelim.)", use 2024 (final) as the last snapshot instead, or show 2025 with a lighter/dashed style.

Things worth knowing

  • 1980's quickest lineup was BMW at 13.1 s. In 2025 the slowest brand total (Subaru, 9.0 s) beats it by 4 seconds.
  • Industry average: 0–60 went from 15.6 s to 7.3 s, horsepower from 104 to 264, and MPG from 19.2 to 28.1, while average weight grew from 3,228 to 4,441 lbs.
  • Mercedes was last in 1980's 0–60 ranking (17.8 s) and is #3 in 2025.
  • Ford and Stellantis are #4 and #5 for 0–60 in 2025 but #12 and #14 for MPG: big, powerful trucks.
  • Check: each brand total matches EPA's own production-weighted blend of its cars and trucks to within 0.11%.
